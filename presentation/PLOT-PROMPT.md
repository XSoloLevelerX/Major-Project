# Plot & Figure Generation Prompt

Everything below is meant to be **copy-pasted into an AI assistant** (Claude, ChatGPT, Gamma,
or the `dataviz` skill) to produce the visuals for the mid-semester presentation.

Two things to know before you paste:

1. **Every number must come from `asd-gnn/results/`.** Do not let a model invent values.
   The prompts below tell it to read the files. If you are pasting into a tool with no file
   access, paste the contents of `results/tables/table_kfold.md`,
   `results/tables/table_loso.md`, `results/qc/cohort.json` and `results/qc/sanity.json`
   alongside the prompt.
2. **Six of the figures already exist**, generated by `python scripts/04_make_figures.py`.
   Use those PNGs directly rather than re-drawing them. The prompts here cover the
   *presentation-only* visuals that the pipeline does not produce (architecture diagrams,
   the protocol-contrast explainer, the progress timeline).

## Section A — The master prompt (paste this first)

> You are producing the figures for a mid-semester progress presentation for an
> undergraduate major project titled **"ASD Classification from Brain Connectivity using
> Graph Neural Networks"** (PEC Semester 7, Major_Group ID_26271, mentor Prof. Amandeep Kaur).
>
> **Project in one line:** we are building a leakage-audited benchmark of graph neural
> networks on the ABIDE-I resting-state fMRI cohort, and reporting results under *both*
> random 10-fold CV and leave-one-site-out (LOSO) evaluation, because the published
> literature reports the optimistic protocol almost exclusively.
>
> **The narrative spine of the deck — every figure must serve this:** the field reports
> 70–90% accuracy under random k-fold; the same methods fall to near chance (52–56%) when
> the test *site* is genuinely unseen. Our contribution is not a bigger number. It is an
> honest measurement of that gap, plus a model designed against it.
>
> **What we actually measured** (use these, not the literature's numbers, in any figure
> showing our results): best k-fold 0.674 balanced accuracy (linear SVM), best LOSO 0.643
> (MLP), and a k-fold → LOSO drop of only 0.00–0.05 — much smaller than the published
> collapse, because our k-fold number was never inflated in the first place. No GNN beat the
> classical baselines. Cohort: 871 subjects, 403 ASD / 468 TC, 20 sites.
>
> **Design constraints for every figure:**
> - Presentation slides, 16:9, viewed on a projector from the back of a room. Minimum
>   effective font size 18pt. No figure may rely on a caption to be legible.
> - Colour-blind-safe palette. Do not encode meaning by colour alone; use shape or
>   direct labels too.
> - One idea per figure. If a figure needs two sentences to explain, split it.
> - No chartjunk: no 3-D bars, no drop shadows, no gradient fills, no gridline clutter.
> - Direct-label series at the end of the line instead of using a legend where possible.
> - Every axis that shows accuracy or balanced accuracy **must include a chance line at
>   0.50**, labelled "chance". This is the single most important convention in the deck.
> - Whenever a mean is shown, show the spread too (std across folds, or per-fold points).
>   A bare mean with no variance is the exact dishonesty this project exists to criticise.
>
> Acknowledge these constraints, then wait for the individual figure requests.

### C1. Pipeline / architecture diagram — **slide 8**

> Draw a clean left-to-right pipeline diagram, as an SVG suitable for a 16:9 slide.
>
> Stages, in order, each as a labelled box:
> 1. **ABIDE-I preprocessed** — 871 subjects, 20 sites, CPAC / filt_noglobal
> 2. **ROI time series** — `(T × R)` per subject. CC200 gives R=200, of which **179 survive
>    QC** (21 parcels are constant and are dropped). Annotate: *T varies by site — 78 to 296
>    timepoints*
> 3. **Connectivity estimation** — Pearson / partial / tangent → `R × R` matrix →
>    Fisher-z → vectorised upper triangle (**15,931 features** at R=179)
> 4. **Graph construction** — branches into three parallel paths (draw as three tracks):
>    - **(A) Brain graph:** node = ROI, edge = thresholded connectivity → graph-level classification
>    - **(B) Population graph:** node = subject, edge = phenotypic similarity → node-level, transductive
>    - **(C) Hybrid:** brain graph → GNN encoder → 64-d subject embedding → population graph node feature
> 5. **Model** — GCN / GAT / GIN / GraphSAGE / ChebNet vs classical SVM / Ridge / RF / MLP
> 6. **Evaluation** — splits into two boxes side by side: *10-fold CV* and *leave-one-site-out*
> 7. **Results JSON on disk** → auto-generated tables and figures
>
> Critical annotation: draw a vertical dashed red line labelled **"fold boundary — everything
> to the right of this is fitted on TRAINING SUBJECTS ONLY"** positioned so that connectivity
> estimation, scaling, feature selection and ComBat all sit inside it. This is the
> methodological point of the whole slide; make it visually dominant.
>
> Style: flat, no shadows, muted greys for the data-flow boxes, one accent colour for the
> three graph-formulation tracks, red only for the leakage boundary. Sans-serif. Minimum
> 18pt effective text.

## Section E — Guardrails to repeat to any model you use

Paste this verbatim whenever you ask a model to touch the numbers:

> Rules for this task, without exception:
> - Every numeric value must be traceable to a file under `asd-gnn/results/`. If you cannot
>   find it there, write `TBD` — never estimate, never interpolate, never carry a number
>   over from a published paper.
> - Balanced accuracy is the primary metric, not accuracy. The cohort is imbalanced.
> - Never present a mean without its standard deviation across folds.
> - Never describe a result on the synthetic cohort as a result on ABIDE. The synthetic
>   cohort has no class signal by construction; it exists only to test the plumbing, and
>   scores near 0.50 there are the *expected* outcome, not a failure.
> - Do not describe this system as diagnostic, clinical, or deployable. It is a research
>   prototype for decision support.
